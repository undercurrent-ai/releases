BreachRadar

Breach monitoring and an outreach queue, on your own machine

Watches SEC EDGAR 8-K Item 1.05 filings and six incident-press feeds, and puts every new disclosure in front of you as a triage queue rather than a dashboard you have to remember to open.

Eight columns from Inbox to Call booked. The day counter on each card is computed as it renders, so a target sitting in Hold turns colour by itself on the morning it enters the window you care about. Nothing is auto-filed into an outbound column — extracting an organisation from a headline is a heuristic and it is wrong often enough to matter.

No daemon, no API keys, no telemetry and no server. It polls when you open it, or once a day at most. Your board, notes and settings stay in a folder in your home directory and an update never touches them.

Breach Radar 0.3.0

The release where the board stops being a list of companies you cannot do anything about.

Send outreach

A bright button in the top bar carrying the number of targets that are writable today — inside the day 45–120 window with no first touch sent. The same button appears again in the article, where you have just finished reading and deciding. Both open a compose sheet:

  • Four editable touches. The clock, the artefact, the workspace, the

forward. The fourth asks for a name rather than a meeting, because a forward to the person who owns the first hour of an incident beats a call with the wrong one.

  • Recipients ranked, best first, by whether they have an address at all and

how close their title is to CISO / Deputy CISO / Head of IR — with Head of Platform as the second door.

  • Four ways to send. The default opens a Gmail compose window in your

browser, signed in as you. The send then comes from a real mailbox on your own domain, carries its SPF, DKIM and DMARC, lands in Sent where replies thread, and cannot turn into a blast by accident. There is also a mailto: route for Mail and Outlook, a copy-for-LinkedIn route, and an automated route that is deliberately not armed.

Two gates, and they both run twice

Eligibility is the day 45–120 window. That is not inbox etiquette — during notification and investigation the people worth reaching are under instruction not to characterise the incident to outsiders. They cannot have the conversation, however good the email is.

Content reads the rendered message and refuses:

  • a first touch that names the incident, or reuses the language of their own

headline — every competitor opens with "I saw the news", and that line is why this category converts at about one percent;

  • a message carrying a claim code without the experimental-use and

satisfy-your-own-standards blocks;

  • credential-harvester phrasing ("click here", "log in here") anywhere near a

code.

Both gates run again on exactly the bytes about to leave, because the draft is editable after it was rendered. The override is there, and it is logged together with the refusal it ignored — a rule that cannot be broken gets worked around invisibly instead.

Rogue agents

A third incident tier, beside "somebody got in" and "the lights went off": an autonomous system did something nobody authorised. It has its own pane, its own filter, three agent-era feeds, and a watch list of standing searches for the cases the security wires miss because they file them under AI news.

The watch list opens searches; it does not assert incidents. The app will not tell you a named company's agent did a named thing it cannot cite.

Existing cards are promoted into the category one way only — never out of a kind you set by hand.

Undercurrent workspaces and claim codes

Reserve <theirorg>.undercurrent.ai for a target and carry the claim code in the third message. Off behind two independent switches: one here, one on the server.

The code is not a credential. Redeeming it requires signing in normally first, at a domain you typed. It attaches an empty workspace to an account that already exists; it never creates one and never logs anybody in.

The store is SQLite now

state.json was the only copy of which targets had been contacted, rewritten whole on every keystroke, and one bad field could make the entire board undecodable. It is not the source of truth any more.

  • Every value is its own column, so a bad one is a default on one row instead

of an empty board.

  • A save is one transaction. A crash rolls back to the last commit rather than

to a half-written file.

  • PRAGMA integrity_check runs on open, and a failure restores the newest

snapshot instead of quietly starting over.

  • Snapshots are taken with VACUUM INTO while the app is live.

Your existing state.json is imported once, never written to again, and never deleted. A portable JSON copy is still written to export/state.json — as a mirror, not as the record.

Your theme survives quitting

Two separate bugs were throwing it away. WKWebView does not persist localStorage for a file:// page, so every theme, scheme and accent choice went into a store that emptied on quit; and the restore path clicked the accent Reset on every launch, discarding a custom accent even when it had survived. Both fixed.

A top bar you can read

Three bands of chrome saying overlapping things became one row: three tabs, the send button, a Filters button that stays shut until asked for, and a status line set at a size meant for reading rather than for captions. Type, fit, grouping and sort moved behind Advanced filters. Theme and scheme moved out of the native toolbar into Settings.

Kanban

Cards in a column had no flex basis, so once a column overflowed every card was compressed below its own content and clipped — which is why the board looked like overlapping half-rows of text. Fixed, and the column scrolls.

The rail

Thirteen rows became eight, in two tiers. Drag them into the order you actually work in. The kanban, the agent pane and the data sources moved into the second, demoted tier. Click counts are kept locally so Sort by use can answer a question that cannot be answered after the fact.
