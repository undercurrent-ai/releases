Foundry

Finds every app you had an AI build, and what it was built from

Build enough small tools and you lose track of them. Foundry is the registry: it scans the places apps actually end up, identifies the ones that are yours, and shows each with its version, its size, what it was built with, and links straight to its source repository and its runtime data folder.

Launch, reveal in Finder, or open the repo in a terminal. Favourites and ordering are yours to set and are remembered.

It identifies apps by bundle identifier prefix rather than by guessing. A heuristic like "small and unsigned" would sweep in half of Applications; an allowlist finds the things you made and nothing else. Extra roots and extra identifiers are settings, so an app in an unusual place can be added without a rebuild.

A caveat worth stating plainly: Foundry resolves each app to its source repository through a table of conventions — where repos live, how project folders are named. Those conventions are the author's. On a machine laid out differently it will still find and launch your apps, but the repo and runtime links will come up empty until you point its extra-roots setting at the right places.
